# Contributing

Thank you for helping make Awesome KyoAni more useful. This is a curated list, not a directory of every Kyoto Animation-related link, so every addition needs a clear reason to be here.

## Quality Requirements

Every submission must meet all of these requirements:

1. **Directly relevant**: Kyoto Animation is central to the resource, not a passing reference, tag, screenshot, or optional theme.
2. **Useful**: The resource serves a recognizable need for viewers, researchers, creators, animation professionals, or the community.
3. **Understandable**: A newcomer can determine what the resource provides and how it is useful.
4. **Distinct**: It adds meaningful value beyond existing entries.
5. **Recommendable**: A contributor can explain why it deserves attention beyond popularity, ratings, follower counts, or search ranking.
6. **Legal to link**: It does not primarily distribute unauthorized copies of animation, music, books, production materials, or other copyrighted works.

Popularity is a signal, not an acceptance criterion. High-quality resources in Japanese, Chinese, Korean, English, and other languages are equally welcome when their value is clear.

## Acceptance Paths

A resource that meets the baseline quality requirements should also fit at least one of these paths:

### Authoritative Source

A first-party or otherwise authoritative resource, such as an official Kyoto Animation website, work or project page, publication, catalog, store, channel, archive, or event resource.

### Established Community Resource

A well-maintained and demonstrably useful community project, database, archive, reference, event, tool, or research resource with a clear track record and a substantial Kyoto Animation focus.

### Exceptional Niche Resource

A specialized resource with unusual research, preservation, technical, historical, educational, or community value that is difficult to replace, even when its audience is small or its activity is infrequent.

These paths are not popularity tiers. A small specialist archive can be more valuable to the list than a widely shared but shallow page.

## Scope

Good fits include:

- Official Kyoto Animation websites, work catalogs, stores, publishing resources, channels, and project pages.
- Databases and indexes for works, staff credits, releases, production information, and related metadata.
- Resources about animation production, direction, key animation, character design, backgrounds, color design, cinematography, compositing, and other production crafts.
- Substantive creator profiles, interviews, career references, and staff research.
- Books, magazines, catalogs, bibliographies, and official or scholarly publications.
- Music catalogs, soundtrack references, composer resources, and audio-production material.
- Pilgrimage maps, location guides, and documented research into real-world settings used in Kyoto Animation works.
- Established communities, events, archives, databases, tools, and reusable fan projects centered on Kyoto Animation.
- Essays, interviews, criticism, academic work, and long-form production research.
- Focused Awesome lists for individual Kyoto Animation works when they are substantial and independently maintained.

Usually out of scope:

- Generic anime recommendation lists or databases that offer no meaningful Kyoto Animation-specific value.
- Personal bookmark dumps, minimally documented projects, one-off exercises, and isolated fan works.
- Individual fan art, edits, clips, memes, wallpapers, and merchandise listings.
- General-purpose software with only a Kyoto Animation skin, example, or optional theme.
- Resources connected only through a creator who once worked at Kyoto Animation when the resource itself is not substantially about the studio or its work.
- Pirated streams, unauthorized downloads, disc rips, scanlations, book or artbook scans, production-material dumps, and reuploads of copyrighted media.
- Referral links, affiliate links, advertisements, and self-promotional spam.

## Studio Connection

A resource should have a clear and verifiable connection to Kyoto Animation. Prefer official work pages, explicit production credits, official publications, reliable staff databases, or other primary and well-established references when establishing that connection.

Do not expand the list to unrelated works simply because a former or current Kyoto Animation creator participated in them. The resource itself should improve understanding of Kyoto Animation, its productions, its creators, or its surrounding community.

## Work-Specific Resources

Use the thematic sections for studio-wide resources and resources that genuinely span multiple Kyoto Animation works. A resource dedicated primarily to one production or franchise belongs under that title in **Works**.

Do not duplicate the same resource in both a thematic section and a work section. When a resource uses one production as a case study but its main value is broader research into Kyoto Animation's production methods, staff, or institutional culture, keep it in the relevant thematic section.

Create a new work subsection only when at least one strong dedicated resource is ready to include. Do not pre-create an empty catalog of every Kyoto Animation title. A substantial independently maintained Awesome list can serve as the anchor resource for a work subsection.

## Official and Community Resources

Prefer canonical official URLs when an official resource is the subject of an entry. Community resources can be included when they provide substantial independent value such as research, indexing, preservation, tooling, or documentation.

Do not duplicate an official source with multiple mirrors unless a mirror has genuine preservation value and is clearly identified as such.

## Languages

The list and all entry descriptions are maintained in English so the catalog stays consistent and searchable. Resources in any language are welcome. When language materially affects usability, mention it factually in the description.

## Historical Resources

Age and inactivity alone are not reasons to remove a resource. Finished or inactive projects can remain when they are still useful and accessible.

Broken, obsolete, superseded, or preservation-only resources may later be moved to a separate historical reference file when keeping them in the main list would make the current recommendations less useful.

## Entry Format

Use the canonical resource name and an HTTPS URL whenever possible. Descriptions must be concise, factual, start with an uppercase character, and end with a period.

```markdown
- [Resource Name](https://example.com/) - What it provides and why it is useful.
```

Keep entries alphabetized within each section unless a section has a documented reason to use another ordering.

## Review Process

Submissions are reviewed for:

1. **Format**: The entry follows the canonical Markdown format and naming conventions.
2. **Placement**: The resource is in the narrowest appropriate section.
3. **Duplicates**: It is not already listed or substantially covered by an existing entry.
4. **Connection**: Its Kyoto Animation relevance is direct and verifiable.
5. **Quality**: It meets every baseline requirement and at least one acceptance path.
6. **Legality**: The linked resource is appropriate to recommend and does not primarily redistribute unauthorized copyrighted material.

A new section should be proposed only when existing sections cannot describe a meaningful cluster of high-quality resources. Include a one-sentence scope description for any new section and update the Contents list at the same time.

## Automatic Rejection

A pull request may be closed without extended review when it:

- Adds multiple unrelated standalone resources in one pull request.
- Duplicates an existing entry or a recently reviewed proposal without explaining a material difference.
- Has an empty, placeholder, or purely promotional pull request description.
- Places the resource in an unrelated category without justification.
- Proposes a resource whose Kyoto Animation connection is incidental or unverifiable.
- Links a resource with no understandable purpose, documentation, or usable content.
- Primarily links piracy, unauthorized media redistribution, scans, rips, or copyrighted-material dumps.
- Exists mainly for advertising, referrals, affiliate traffic, or self-promotion without independent value.

Inactivity, age, archive status, or a small audience are not automatic rejection reasons. Historical and specialist resources are judged by continuing usefulness and irreplaceability rather than software-style activity metrics.

## Pull Request Checklist

Before opening a pull request:

- Search the README, issues, and open and recently closed pull requests for duplicates.
- Read or inspect the resource rather than relying only on its title, description, stars, follower counts, or search snippet.
- Confirm that Kyoto Animation is central to the resource.
- State which acceptance path best explains why the resource belongs in the list.
- Use the canonical HTTPS URL; prefer the primary source or official project page.
- Add the entry to the narrowest appropriate section.
- Explain the intended users and why the resource stands out from existing entries.
- Disclose whether you maintain or are affiliated with the submitted resource.
- Confirm that the resource does not primarily provide unauthorized copyrighted material.
- Check spelling, grammar, Markdown formatting, and alphabetical order.
- Keep the pull request focused. One standalone resource per pull request is preferred.

Maintainers may edit descriptions, move entries, request stronger evidence, or decline submissions that do not improve the list.
